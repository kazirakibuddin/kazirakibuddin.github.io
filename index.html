About

I am a Graduate Research Assistant in the Water Quality Lab at Kentucky State University, working with Dr. Andrew J. Ray on a USDA Evans-Allen funded project. Most of my days involve building and maintaining recirculating systems, sampling fish, and keeping water quality in check across several culture systems. My thesis trial compares salt types and salinity levels for olive flounder (Paralichthys olivaceus), measuring growth, survival and gene expression by qPCR.

My interest in feeds started at Sylhet Agricultural University in Bangladesh. There I ran a feeding trial on climbing perch using fermented mustard oil cake and fermented soybean meal as replacements for conventional protein, and presented the work at Aquaculture America 2025 in New Orleans. I also worked on black soldier fly larvae oil as a lipid source for butter catfish, and contributed to a 2026 paper in Animals on terrestrial oils as fish oil replacements in Gangetic catfish.

I am now looking for PhD and research positions where nutrition, fish physiology and production systems meet. At KSU I also serve as Vice President of the U.S. Aquaculture Society Student Subunit.

Featured publication

Evaluation of Different Terrestrial Oils as an Alternative to Dietary Fish Oil on Feed Physical Properties, Growth, Feed Utilization, and Fatty Acid Profile of Gangetic Catfish (Mystus cavasius)

A 70-day feeding trial comparing fish oil with soybean, black soldier fly larvae, palm and mixed oils. The mixed-oil diet gave the best growth, and feed efficiency did not differ among diets.
